How tournaments work

A tournament is a set of Klaxon rooms run together: one packet list, one roster, one scoring format, and one set of stats. You (the director) run it from the director console; moderators read in MODAQ; players use the ordinary Klaxon buzzer.

The two kinds, and who does what

Teams or shootout

  • Teams: scheduled games between teams, in as many rooms as you need. You get rosters, round packets you release one at a time, live stats, errata and protests.
  • Shootout: everyone plays for themselves, made with Discord nights in mind. It has a buzz queue, typed answers and a chat, and scores can carry across packets. It starts with one room. See Shootouts.

You choose the kind when you create the tournament, and you can't change it afterwards.

Who's who

  • Director: creates the tournament and runs the console. The console is at /t/CODE, where CODE is the 5-character tournament code.
  • Moderator (reader): reads one room in MODAQ and scores it. They get in with that room's reader link, or with a Klaxon account you've approved.
  • Co-reader: a second person in the same game, for example a scorekeeper. They can score buzzes and bonus parts too.
  • Players: join a room with its code or player link and buzz.

Before the day

1. Create the tournament

Sign in to a Klaxon account first. If you create the tournament while signed in, your account can open the director console on any device. If you create it signed out, only the browser you used holds the director key, so don't clear that browser's site data.

On Create a tournament, fill in:

  • Name and date. Leave List on Klaxon's public tournament directory ticked if you want moderators browsing Tournaments to find you.
  • Scoring format: the tossup point scheme, whether there are bonuses, the players' live scoresheet, whether to show categories and question text after they're read, and Record every buzz, which also keeps the buzzes that lost the race. Turn that last one on before play starts, because buzzes aren't kept while it's off.
  • Round packets, roster and default room settings. These are all optional here, and all can be done later in the console.

Once it's created, use Edit settings in the console to change the name, date, listing, scoring and bonus format, scoresheet and question display, buzz recording, the default settings for new rooms, and shootout options. The one thing you can't change is Teams vs Shootout.

2. Upload packets

Packets are uploaded on the console's Packets & roster tab, as MODAQ .json files. You can select several at once.

  • Each file's name becomes the round's label. Name them simply, e.g. 1.json, 2.json.
  • For Word files, convert them first with the packet parser. It also checks the packet for common problems.
  • A new round starts hidden. Moderators can't see it until you release it.
  • Drag rounds by their handle to put them in order. The order sets each round's number, what moderators see, and which round is released next.
  • Upload spare questions with tiebreaker ticked. They become a pool that moderators can sub in. Split turns a tiebreaker round into one round per question, so you can release them one at a time.

By default, moderators need a director-approved account to load the tournament's packets, even if they have a reader link. You can switch that off when you create the tournament.

3. Add the roster

Copy the rows from your registration spreadsheet, header row included (Team Name, Player 1, Player 2…). Click Paste or edit roster and paste them in. You can fix names, add a player or remove a team, then click Save roster. Uploading a registration .qbj also works.

MODAQ fills its teams from this roster. By default, players also pick their team and name from it when they join. Anyone who joins under a name that isn't on the roster is listed under Players not on the roster so you can follow up.

4. Make the rooms

On the Rooms tab, enter a name prefix and a number and click Add. You get "Room 1", "Room 2" and so on. Each room has three links:

  • Players (/r/CODE): for the teams.
  • Reader: private, for that room's moderator.
  • Co-reader: private, for a second scorer.

Copy for spreadsheet copies every room's links as rows you can paste into a sheet. New rooms start with the tournament's default room settings, and each room's moderator can still change their own room's options.

5. Line up moderators

You have two options:

  • Hand out reader links from the Rooms tab, one per room. This is quick and needs no accounts, but if the tournament requires approved accounts, the moderator still has to sign in with one to load packets.
  • Approve accounts. On Moderators & links, copy the Moderator invite link (/t/CODE/join) and send it to your moderators. They sign in or create an account and click Request to moderate. Their requests show up in that same panel, where you click Approve (or Deny). If your account has an email address, Klaxon also emails you about each request. You can also add someone directly by their email or username. Once approved, a moderator can read in any room of the tournament without a reader link: the invite page lists the rooms, each with a Read here button.

Players who read on their byes. Send them the second link on that panel, Player reading during a bye round (/t/CODE/join?as=bye), or add them with Add bye reader. They come in as Assigned rounds only with no rounds, so approving them lets them read nowhere yet. Then add each round and room they read under their name. They see only those packets, and only while their room is idle or on their round. In every other round they're a player. Their invite page lists just their rounds. Assigned readers by round, under the list, shows any round with nobody assigned. A reader link handed to them still opens everything, so give them the invite link instead.

6. Links for players

On Moderators & links:

  • Player links: a schedule URL and a Discord invite. They're shown to players in every room.
  • Player landing page: a secret link to one page with every room and its code, the live stats, and your schedule and Discord links. The tournament code alone doesn't open it.

On the day

Release each round

Moderators can only load rounds you've released. On Packets & roster:

  • Release next packet releases the first hidden round in your list. The button names the round it will release.
  • To release or re-hide a particular round, tick or untick its Visible box.
  • With auto-release when a round finishes on, Klaxon releases the next round by itself once every room that has played has a finished game for the current round.

What moderators do

  1. Open the reader link, or Read here on the invite page. If the tournament requires approved accounts, sign in first.
  2. In the setup screen, pick the released round under Packet and click Start reading.
  3. On Set up the game, choose the two teams from the roster and tick who plays for each (unticked players start on the bench and can sub in later). Put any connected player who isn't on a team yet on theirs, then click Start the game. Players see Starting soon above their buzzer meanwhile.
  4. Read. Buzzes appear in the Klaxon buzz panel beside the question, labelled with the player's name, and scores go to the director's console as the moderator reads.
  5. When the moderator reaches the last question, the game counts as finished. MODAQ's Finish & save to Klaxon export also finishes it, even if the game ended early.
  6. Next game goes back to the setup screen for the next round and teams. The room stays open across rounds, and players don't have to rejoin.

If a moderator's laptop dies, they can open the same link on another device and the game resumes where it was.

What players do

  • Open their room's player link, or type the room code on the Klaxon home page. Then pick their team and name from the roster.
  • Players who arrive early for a later game see a grey NEXT GAME buzzer. It turns on when the moderator starts the game with their team in it.
  • Players see a live scoresheet of the questions already read, unless you've turned it off. From any played row they can choose Protest this question.
  • If the buzzer looks stuck, a player can tap Buzzer isn't clear — alert the moderator.

Keeping in touch

  • Message readers (Rooms tab) sends a note to one room's moderator or to all of them, for example an erratum or "hold for the last room". Players never see these messages, and a moderator who connects later still gets them.
  • Games in progress (Stats tab) shows every room's score, which question it's on, and how long it has been running.
  • When a player lodges a protest, it appears under Results → Protests right away. Turn on Notify me there to get a browser notification for each new one.

Errata and tiebreakers

Moderators log errata and thrown-out questions from MODAQ's errata tools. Both appear on your Results tab. When a moderator uses a tiebreaker question, Tiebreakers used on the Packets tab records which teams have already heard it.

After the games

Results

The Results tab shows every game, grouped by round, as it's played. Click a room to download that game's .qbj; the Stats tab has the whole tournament's downloads. Each game has two buttons:

  • Edit opens the game in MODAQ in a private correction room. Use it to move a buzz to the right player or fix a ruling. Changes save straight back into the stats.
  • Remove leaves the game out of standings, box scores and downloads, for example a test game or a forfeit. It stays saved, and Restore puts it back.

If you fixed games in YellowFruit instead, use Import YellowFruit file. Games that match by round and teams replace the stored ones, and new games are added.

Protests

A protest becomes something you can rule on once the moderator records it in MODAQ and the game is final. Click Rule… and pick a resolution: denied, answer accepted, or question thrown out. Klaxon shows exactly what would change in the score before you apply it. If the resolution means a question still has to be played, use Apply and open a replay room. You get a reader link and a player link to bring both teams back.

Stats and downloads

  • Public stats link (Stats tab, /t/CODE/stats) shows live standings, individual stats and box scores, without answer lines. Anyone can open it, and the tournament code on its own (klaxonbuzz.com/CODE) goes there too.
  • Tournament structure: group rounds into phases (Prelims, Playoffs) and teams into divisions, so the stats are split the way YellowFruit splits them.
  • Download YellowFruit report (.zip) is the stats as HTML pages. Download for YellowFruit (Packets tab) gives you the whole tournament as a .yft file to open in YellowFruit, plus the roster as a .qbj.
  • Buzzpoints shows every scored buzz with its answer line, so it's for you only. You can open it as a report, as CSV, or as a zip laid out for quizbowlbuzzpoints.com. Turn on record every buzz attempt below them and the zip also carries the buzzes that lost the race, as klaxon_all_buzz_attempts.json (only ones made while it was on).
  • Temporary share links let you share the stats or buzzpoints with someone for 7 days. You can revoke a link at any time.

MASSINGER (pick/ban) tournaments

Before each game, the teams take turns in a fixed order. The first team bans. Then the second team bans and protects, then the first team bans and protects, and so on, until each team has made 3 bans. The last ban ends it. From a 26-tossup packet that leaves 20, and the game is read on those. The banned tossups become the tiebreakers, in their packet order: if the game is tied after 20, question 21 is the first tossup that was banned.

Set up

  1. Create the tournament as a team tournament. Under the scoring format, tick MASSINGER pick/ban before each game, then choose who picks (each team's captain, anyone on the team, or the moderator only) and the timer (seconds per pick, 0 for none).
  2. Check the packets. Each round needs more than 20 tossups (26 uses all six bans), and every tossup needs its subcategory as the last part of its metadata, after the last comma (<Author, Science - Biology>), with at least two different subcategories. A packet that doesn't qualify is read in full, without a pick/ban.
  3. Upload the roster (Packets & roster). Moderators then choose teams from it instead of typing names. With roster joining on, players also pick their team and name when they join, so they're already on the right team.
  4. Make the rooms and line up moderators as usual (above).

Each game

  1. Release the round. The moderator picks it on the setup screen and clicks Start reading.
  2. Teams. The same Set up the game screen as any tournament game: the two teams, who plays, and every connected player on a team (all of them, since players make the picks), plus a captain for each team if captains pick. Then Continue to pick/ban.
  3. Pick/ban. Who picks first is a coin flip unless the moderator chooses a team. The moderator clicks Begin pick/ban, and players pick on their own screens. When the timer runs out, a random pick of the due kind (ban or protect) is made. The moderator can undo or edit picks.
  4. Read. Continue to the game starts the game straight away with the lobby's teams and players. The pick/ban result stays folded above the game.

Afterwards

Stats work as usual. The buzzpoints download puts every buzz on the right question of the full packet, even though each room read a different 20. If a packet was edited after a game was played on it, klaxon_numbering_warnings.json in the zip lists the games to check.

Shootouts

A shootout tournament works mostly like a team one, with these differences:

  • No teams or roster. Every player is their own team. Whoever opens the room's link is in the game. With Add players to MODAQ as they buzz in on (the default), a player who joins mid-packet plays from the next question.
  • You set the rules once, when you create the shootout: the scoring, whether bonuses are read, the score carrying across packets, typed answers, how withdrawing a buzz works, and notes for the players. Each room's moderator sees these and confirms them.
  • One room to start. It's created with the tournament. With only one room, every packet you upload is ready for it straight away, in your list order, with nothing to release. If you add a second room, you release rounds one at a time, as in a team tournament.
  • The console is smaller. The Packets tab only has packets, and there's no Stats tab. The score is the room's leaderboard, and players chat beside the buzzer.

Questions

I opened the console on another computer and can't change anything.

The console's director tools need the director key. Sign in with the account you used when you created the tournament, or the account you had signed in the first time you opened the console, and the console picks the key up from that account. Without an account, only the browser that created the tournament has the key.

A moderator says there are no packets.

Either the round isn't released yet (check Visible on the Packets tab), or the tournament requires approved accounts and they aren't signed in to one you've approved. Look under Moderators & links → Reader accounts.

A moderator shared their own address bar with the players. Can players now moderate?

No. Klaxon removes the private part of a reader link from the address bar, so what a moderator copies from it is only the player link. Reader and co-reader links come from your console, and you hand them out.

A team turned up in the wrong room.

They're shown NEXT GAME until the moderator starts a game with their team, so they can't buzz into someone else's game. Point them to the right room from the player landing page, or from the room list shown in every room.

Somebody's score is wrong in a finished game.

Use Edit on that game under Results. To leave the game out of the stats entirely, use Remove, which you can undo.

Can I close a tournament room between rounds?

No need. Tournament rooms can't be ended, because the next round reads in the same room. They stay open for the whole tournament.
